Behind the Screens

Asian American Subgroups Across Tech-Related Fields

Lawrence Novilla and Nimai Kasibatla
CSE 194, UC San Diego, Fall 2026

Reports on education, income and jobs, including jobs in tech, usually list Asian Americans as a single group. That one figure combines people whose families come from many different countries, and it can hide large differences between those subgroups. This site places the combined figure next to the figures for each subgroup, using published data from the sources listed at the end of the page.

Why the groups get combined

Federal standards for race data define Asian as one of a few broad minimum categories, and many surveys and reports publish results only at that level. Smaller groups also have few people in any single year of survey data, which makes their estimates less reliable. Pew Research Center handles this by pooling three years of American Community Survey data, 2021 to 2023, for its group fact sheets (S2, S4). Regular reports on jobs tend to stay broader. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports only the largest Asian groups separately and places the rest in an Other Asian category (S6), and the tech and STEM job figures we found, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Pew Research Center and the National Science Board, report Asian workers as one group (S7, S9, S10).

A combined figure puts everyone in one pool, so the largest groups shape it the most. Groups outside the six largest make up of the Asian American population (S1).
